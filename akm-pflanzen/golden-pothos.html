Golden Pothos (Epipremnum aureum)

Description

Golden Pothos, also known as Devil's Ivy, is one of the most popular and resilient houseplants available. It's a trailing vine with heart-shaped leaves, typically green variegated with splashes of yellow or gold. Extremely adaptable and easy to care for, it thrives in various indoor conditions and can be grown in pots, hanging baskets, or trained to climb. It's also known for its air-purifying capabilities.

Care Instructions

  • Light: Highly adaptable. Prefers bright, indirect light which maintains its variegation. Can tolerate lower light conditions, but variegation may fade. Avoid direct, intense sunlight which can burn the leaves. Suitable for many spots in a typical Munich apartment.
  • Water: Water thoroughly when the top 1-2 inches (2-5 cm) of soil feel dry. Allow excess water to drain out. Pothos is quite forgiving if you occasionally forget to water, but avoid letting it sit in waterlogged soil.
  • Soil: Use a standard, well-draining potting mix.
  • Temperature: Comfortable in average room temperatures between 18-29°C (65-85°F). Protect from cold drafts, especially near windows in winter.
  • Humidity: Tolerates average indoor humidity but appreciates slightly higher levels. Misting occasionally or placing it in a bathroom or kitchen can be beneficial.
  • Fertilizer: Feed sparingly with a balanced liquid houseplant fertilizer diluted to half-strength once every 1-2 months during the spring and summer growing season (roughly April to September). No need to fertilize in fall and winter.
  • Pruning: Prune as needed to maintain shape, encourage bushier growth, or control size. Cuttings root very easily in water or soil.

Fun Facts

  • Common Names: Devil's Ivy (reportedly because it's hard to kill and stays green even in low light), Money Plant, Ceylon Creeper, Hunter's Robe.
  • Origin: Native to Mo'orea in the Society Islands of French Polynesia, but now naturalized in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide.
  • Air Purifier: Like the Peace Lily, Pothos is noted for its ability to remove indoor air pollutants such as formaldehyde, benzene, and xylene.
  • Confusion: Often confused with heartleaf Philodendron; Pothos leaves are generally thicker and more textured.
  • Vigorous Grower: Under ideal conditions, its vines can grow quite long.

Toxicity

Caution: Golden Pothos contains insoluble calcium oxalate crystals. It is toxic to cats, dogs, and humans if ingested. Chewing or biting can cause oral irritation, pain and swelling of mouth, tongue and lips, excessive drooling, vomiting, and difficulty swallowing.
